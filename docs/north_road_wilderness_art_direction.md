# North Road Wilderness Art Direction

The North Road is readable overcast wilderness, not a fog-hidden void. Its mood comes from physical geography: road cuts against redwood slopes, wet lowland banks, enclosed ritual trees, exposed ridge wind, the Bent Road's repeated silhouettes, a root-compressed ravine, and the hard military geometry of the fort approach.

Materials remain legible and near-neutral. Dry ridge grass, matted lowland grass, worn roadside grass, pebbly packed earth, churned ford mud, wet dark creek mud, cracked seasonal mud, dark cliff rock, aged timber, rusted hardware, and restrained stone ruins are assigned by land use and drainage—not random variety. Source textures stay untouched.

Black growth is wet plant fiber, oily scab, tarred root, and burnt mycelium. It physically grips roadwork and terrain. It is never smoke, currency, gore, purple corruption, slime, or a featureless black blob.

Sector silhouettes progress from familiar Folsom road posts to redwood walls, a hollow camp shelf, pale willow creek banks, ritual cypress, sparse ridge trees, controlled repeated Bent Road landmarks, a root-heavy ravine, then straighter retaining walls and the distant Empty Fort mass. Fog is restrained and localized; it never conceals missing terrain or unreadable paths.
